Pei-Ching Chan

I am a Master's researcher in Biotechnology at National Taiwan University, currently broadening my research horizons as an exchange student at the University of Helsinki. My expertise lies at the intersection of synthetic biology and functional biomaterials, with a specific focus on engineering microbial systems to solve sustainability challenges.

With a strong academic foundation (GPA 4.16/4.30), my research has focused on the biosynthesis of red pigments and the development of porous cellulose fiber. I specialize in genetic engineering of E. coli for macromolecule production, microbial fermentation optimization across bacteria, yeast, and fungi, and bio-based materials built from agro-waste and bacterial cellulose nanofibers.

I hold a BS in Food Safety from Taipei Medical University, where I led a national research project on microbial leather production and served as President of the Food Safety Student Association. I previously worked as a Research Assistant Intern at Jheng Han Biotechnology, focused on agrochemical formulation R&D.

I thrive in interdisciplinary, multicultural environments — serving as a trilingual bridge (Mandarin / English / Japanese) for international lab collaborators — and I am always open to discussing synthetic biology, fermentation science, or potential collaborations in the Helsinki ecosystem.
